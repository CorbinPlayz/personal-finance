# Fix Log

Every weird problem that got fixed, written down. This is the portfolio, the résumé, and the ad — all the same file.

The rule: **one entry per job.** Not one entry per success. A fix you couldn't complete is still an entry, because "here's what everyone else tried and here's what I ruled out" is the thing that makes people trust you with the next one.

## Why this exists

Nobody hands the interesting problem to a stranger. They hand it to whoever fixed the last weird thing. You cannot be "known for fixing hard problems" without a list of hard problems you've fixed — so the list is the first asset, and it costs nothing but the habit of writing it down.

It also does something less obvious: by entry ~10 you can see your own pattern. Six of them are the same category? That's your specialty. That's what you start charging more for.

## How to write an entry

Copy `TEMPLATE.md`, or copy entry 001. Keep it short — five minutes, not fifty. People reading it care about the middle section, not your prose.

The one section that matters most is **"What everyone else said."** Because "I fixed a printer" is worth nothing, and "three shops said replace the board, it was a $2 thermistor" is worth an hour of somebody's time and a phone call.

## Rules

- **Write it while it's fresh.** Same day. Details evaporate by the next morning.
- **One photo minimum.** Before, after, or the part that failed. Photos make it real to a stranger.
- **Include the dead ends.** "Tried X, didn't help" is proving you're honest, and it saves the next person a wasted hour.
- **Never inflate it.** If it was a five-minute fix, say five minutes and say *why* it was hard to find. The value is the diagnosis, not the labor.
- **Log the failures too.** Mark them `UNRESOLVED` and write down what you'd try next. That's a referral, not an embarrassment — "this one's beyond me, but here's exactly where I got to" is what a professional says.

## Where these get posted

Once an entry is written, it's free marketing that doesn't require you to talk to anybody:

- The exact question in the local Facebook group or subreddit where someone has the same problem
- Model-specific forums (r/Ender3, r/3Dprinting, r/techsupport, the manufacturer's own community)
- The person who referred the job — send them the link, they'll forward it
- Eventually, a page on your offer site, newest first
